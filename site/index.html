How to read the colours

The colours are CIHI's, not ours. CIHI compares the hospital's risk-adjusted result with the Canadian average and tests whether the difference is statistically significant given the number of cases. Green is above average performance, slate grey is no different from average, red is below average performance.

Slate is the common result and is not a problem to solve, which is why it is a neutral grey rather than an amber warning. A hospital this size has few cases for some indicators, so the confidence interval is wide and only a large gap reaches significance. One indicator is left out of the dashboard entirely: CIHI suppressed 30-day stroke in-hospital mortality for 2025–2026 because there were too few cases to report, so there is no result to grade. The Canadian figure for it was 10.3 deaths per 100 and the Ontario figure 9.1, for reference.

Reading the trends

Each card carries CIHI's own trend verdict and a spark line of every year CIHI publishes, with the Canadian average behind it as a faint dashed line. Where a QIP target measures the same thing, it is drawn across the spark line as a finer dotted line, so the hospital's own commitment and the national average can be read against the history at once. The verdict compares the latest year with the hospital's own history, so weakening means this hospital moved away from its past performance, not that it fell behind other hospitals.

HSMR was re-baselined by CIHI in 2026, so the whole series is restated on the new basis and older published HSMR figures will not match. Risk-adjusted rates are also revised when CIHI updates its models, which is why these figures can differ from numbers quoted in earlier reports.

Targets and comparisons are different tests

A CIHI colour says how this hospital compares with other Canadian hospitals. A QIP colour says whether it reached a number it set for itself. The emergency department wait to physician is the one indicator that carries both, and they agree: CIHI calls it below average performance and the QIP has it well short of a 4.50 hour target drawn from regional partner performance. Elsewhere the two can part ways, so neither is read as a check on the other.

The QIP sets no tolerance band, so at target, within 10% and gap to close are a local convention: at target is at or past the committed figure, within 10% is a remaining gap no larger than a tenth of the target value, and anything beyond that is a gap to close. Because a QIP target is written to sit ahead of the baseline, most commitments start in that last band by design, and the number that matters is the one in the gap line on each card. QIP figures are left exactly as published rather than recalculated, which is why the wait to physician reads 7.40 hours for December 2024 to November 2025 in the QIP section and 7.60 hours for 2025–2026 in CIHI's series. Same NACRS measure, different periods, not a discrepancy.
